## Description (copy-paste)
Most of what gets sold as "ancestral living" is guilt wrapped in burlap: make everything yourself, buy nothing, become a one-woman farm by Tuesday.

This isn't that. I spent twelve years chasing that version and it left me exhausted, not connected. What I eventually learned is that our ancestors didn't do everything themselves. They supported the soap maker, the baker, the herbalist. They put their effort where it counted and relied on each other for the rest. This guide is about knowing the difference.

**What's inside (15 pages):**
- In the Kitchen — the daily food foundation, sauerkraut and sourdough step by step, why butter and tallow beat seed oils every time
- Grow Something — two crops anyone can start with, even if you've never touched dirt
- Body & Household — one cleaner for most jobs, what to make versus what to buy, and why your husband's skin might be reacting to "natural" soap
- Know Where You Come From — Old Norse, ancestral healing, and old worldviews that still hold up
- Learn Real Skills, Be Present, Joy That Doesn't Monetize, and We Need Each Other

There are no soap recipes. No instructions for grinding flour. No pressure. Just the handful of old ways that actually earn their place in a modern life — and permission to skip the rest.
