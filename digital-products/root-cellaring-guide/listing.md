## Description (copy-paste)
You don't need a stone-lined underground room to store produce through the winter. You need a cold spot, some damp sand, and the willingness to check on things once a week.

This guide covers how to store vegetables for months using the cold spots you already have — basement corners, garages, buried coolers, crisper drawers, and north-facing closets. No electricity, no special equipment, no construction project. Just temperature, humidity, darkness, and airflow — the same physics that kept food fresh for thousands of years before refrigeration existed.

**What's inside (10 pages):**
- How root cellaring actually works — the four things every storage space needs and why
- What stores well — 14 crops with exact temperature, humidity, and storage life for each
- Six modern alternatives ranked by how well they work — from basement corner to buried cooler
- Sand storage method — the technique that keeps carrots crisp for six months
- Harvesting for storage — curing, handling, and sorting so your produce doesn't rot in week two
- Weekly checks and troubleshooting — what shriveling, sprouting, green patches, and off-smells mean and how to fix them
- Quick-reference table for 14 crops and a supplies checklist

**This is not** a construction manual. It won't tell you how to dig a root cellar or build vent systems. It's for people who want to store their garden harvest or farmers' market haul using what they already have — a basement, a garage, a cooler, a fridge.
