## Description (copy-paste)
We're constantly being told to change our lives. New habits, new products, new routines. The volume of advice we consume leads to failure — not because we're not trying hard enough, but because no one can implement twenty new things at once.

This guide is about subtraction. Fewer products. Fewer inputs. Fewer voices. The goal isn't minimalism as an aesthetic — it's making space for what actually works.

**What's inside (9 pages):**
- The Pressure to Change — why we keep adding and wonder why life feels heavier
- In the Kitchen — mason jars as your everything, fewer cooking oils, one brewer instead of a collection, stainless steel and beeswax wraps over disposables
- Bath & Body — bar soap over pump dispensers, free & clear (no scents, no colors), one moisturizer or two, one essential oil, and why DIY soap experiments aren't worth your Saturday
- Around the House — shop cloths instead of paper towels, skip the dryer sheets, fewer cleaning products under the sink
- The Diaper Bag Rule — why one good thing that does many jobs beats five specialized ones (and yes, there's an actual argument for buying a diaper bag instead of a purse)
- The Noise — journal your top three priorities, unfollow everyone who doesn't serve them, follow only one source per interest
- The DIY Trap — you don't have to make everything, handmade isn't automatically more sustainable, and supporting good brands is not a failure of principles
- Cross-references to six other Kitchen & Kettle guides for going deeper

**This is not** a workbook or a planner. It's a guide to read, sit with, and return to when life feels cluttered. No fill-in-the-blanks, no tracking pages — just practical philosophy from someone who's done the experimenting so you don't have to.
