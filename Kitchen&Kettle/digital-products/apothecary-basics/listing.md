## Description (copy-paste)
A beginner's guide to making your own herbal preparations at home. No background in herbalism required — just a few jars, some dried herbs, and the willingness to let things sit in a dark cabinet for a few weeks.

Covers thirteen preparation methods with clear, practical instructions: teas and infusions, decoctions, tinctures, oxymels, infused honey, medicinal syrups, infused oils, salves, poultices, compresses, steams, infused baths, and witch hazel. Each method tells you what it's best for, when to pick it over another, and exactly how to do it — supplies, ratios, timing, and troubleshooting if something goes wrong.

**What's inside (17 pages):**
- Which method for what — a quick-reference table so you know whether to steep, simmer, or soak
- Full instructions for all thirteen preparation types, with honest notes throughout
- Supplies checklist with clickable checkboxes — track what you have and what you need
- Troubleshooting: what to do when a tincture smells off, an oil goes rancid, or a salve won't set
- Notes page for tracking everything you've made

**This is not** a comprehensive herbalism course. It won't tell you which herbs treat which conditions. It teaches you the methods — the how and the why — so you can apply them to whatever herbs you work with. It's the kitchen skills of a home apothecary.
