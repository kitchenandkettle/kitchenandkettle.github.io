How to keep this almanac

02

This is a place to notice what happened here. It does not need to be complete, polished, or written on schedule.

Start with the year

On the opening page, write down the dates and conditions you already know. First frost. Last frost. When the swallows returned. When the first tomato ripened. The week the well pump failed. The winter the hens stopped laying.

Come back when you have something to say

Use the seasonal pages for a few lines at a time. A storm that changed the garden. A repair that held. A variety worth planting again. A flock behaving strangely. A week when everything came in at once.

Let the record be ordinary

Ten years from now, the small things will be the useful things. What froze. What sprouted early. What you stopped buying. What worked better than expected. What you will never do that way again.

Keep it yours

Write in shorthand, draw a map, tape in a seed packet, or leave a page blank. This is not a performance of a good homestead. It is a record of the place you actually live.

Type directly into the fields and your entries save automatically in this browser. Or print blank pages and write by hand. Use “Save a copy” before moving to a new device.

There is no correct pace

Some years will fill quickly. Some will have only a few lines. A few true details are enough to make a record worth keeping.

Keep what happened here.

A life on a place is made of recurring things and one-time things: the same first frost, a different storm, a repair that finally held, a harvest you still talk about. Write down enough to recognize the shape of it later.
