# Experiment 01: The Warm Cup

## Question this tests

Do people respond to old-world nourishment translated into a realistic workday habit?

## Working title

The Warm Cup: Old Nourishment for a Modern Workday

## Format

A short piece of 800–1,200 words. It is not a product, recipe collection, or medical guide.

## Core idea

People have long used warm, simple drinks and soft foods as practical nourishment: broths, porridges, spiced drinks, teas, and similar preparations. A modern version does not need to recreate a historical kitchen. It needs to preserve the useful feeling of warmth, ease, and regular care in a form someone can make after work.

The modern example may be a broth-based hot chocolate, but the piece must clearly label that as a modern reconstruction rather than present it as an old historical recipe.

## Suggested structure

1. Open with the modern problem: wanting something nourishing after a long workday without cooking a full meal.
2. Describe one documented old-world practice involving a warm drink or soft food.
3. Separate what is known from what is being reconstructed.
4. Explain what the practice practically offered: warmth, calories, hydration, ease, preservation, comfort, or a way to use what was already available.
5. Show the modern version.
6. Give a ten-minute workday version.
7. Add an honest note about what it does not do. Avoid claims about boosting immunity, curing illness, detoxing, or replacing meals or medical care.
8. End with the question: what old practice would be worth carrying into an ordinary Tuesday?

## Research needed

- One reliable historical source for the old drink or soft food
- One source for the relevant ingredient history, if needed
- Current food-safety guidance for broth storage and reheating
- Any safety notes related to ingredients used in the modern version

## What to make

Make one modern version in the smallest reasonable batch. Record:

- Preparation time
- Whether it tastes good enough to repeat
- Whether the ingredients are easy to keep on hand
- Whether it works after a full workday
- What would make someone choose it over ordinary hot chocolate or a mug of broth

## Voice

Plain, curious, practical, and honest. Do not call the reader back to a fantasy of becoming an old-world person. Show how one small practice can fit into a modern life.

## Review after drafting

- Was the subject genuinely interesting to research?
- Did the modern version feel useful rather than gimmicky?
- Does this clearly belong to Wise Woman rather than Kitchen & Kettle?
- Would someone with a full-time job recognize the problem?
- Does the piece make one specific promise?
- What question does it naturally lead to next?
