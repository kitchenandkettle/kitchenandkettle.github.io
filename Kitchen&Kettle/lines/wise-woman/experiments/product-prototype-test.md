# Product Prototype Test

## Goal

Find out whether a practice-based kit is enjoyable and manageable to make before building a product line.

## Prototypes

- One Apothecary Practice Kit
- One Forager's Field Notes Kit
- Ten identical label or observation-card sets

## Test each prototype against

### Making

- Can I make three without losing interest?
- Can I batch the work in one short session?
- Are any steps fiddly, messy, or hard to repeat?
- Does the finished piece look intentional rather than homemade in a bad way?

### Operations

- How many components are required?
- How much shelf or bin space does one batch need?
- Can everything ship flat in a durable mailer?
- What needs special packaging?
- What happens if one component is missing or damaged?

### Customer use

- Is it obvious what to do with the kit?
- Does it invite participation without requiring a class?
- Does it feel useful after the first day?
- Does the kit make a clear promise without medical claims?

### Content

- Can the making process create natural photos or short videos?
- Can one kit produce several content angles?
- Does the product look like it belongs to the wider world of the brand?

## Stop conditions

Do not add a product if it:

- Requires liquid shipping
- Requires medical or therapeutic claims
- Needs frequent custom work after purchase
- Has more than five meaningful components
- Takes longer to assemble than its likely price can support
- Is attractive but does not give the customer a clear practice

## Decision after testing

Choose one:

- Continue to a ten-unit private test
- Simplify the prototype
- Keep the idea as content only
- Drop it
