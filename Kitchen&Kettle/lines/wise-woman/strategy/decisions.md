# Decisions

## Working name
Wise Woman. This is the working name for a Kitchen & Kettle line. Do not lock the public presentation or product naming yet.

## Phase
Exploration. The project is not yet a business.

## Rule for scope
One small experiment at a time. Capture ideas without turning every idea, subject, or component into a product, series, or obligation.

## Rule for truth
Never make a claim sound older, more Norse, more universal, or more medically established than the evidence allows.

## Rule for voice
Learning alongside the audience is a strength, not an apology. Curiosity is more credible than performed certainty.

## Core distinction
The Wise Woman line is about how people have understood, practiced, recorded, and carried knowledge through time. It is not another homesteading shop or a darker version of Kitchen & Kettle.

Kitchen & Kettle owns practical modern homekeeping: what works now. Wise Woman owns the investigation of old knowledge: what is documented, what is uncertain, what can be reconstructed, and what still has meaning or use.

The working phrase is **the annotated old ways**. “Lore” is the method and editorial lens, not a category limited to fermentation, dyes, or household crafts.

## Old Norse layer
Old Norse language, phrases, gods, stories, and associations are a meaningful layer of the brand, not a costume applied to everything. Use them when they genuinely clarify or deepen the subject. Do not force a reference because it sounds atmospheric.

Verify Old Norse connections as carefully as any other historical claim. Distinguish surviving evidence from later interpretation, modern reconstruction, and personal meaning. A product or piece of content does not need a Norse reference to belong in the brand.

## Religious and cultural focus
The project prioritizes pre-Christian pagan traditions and the older practices beneath later Christian layers. Christian adaptations may be discussed when they help explain what changed, survived, or was renamed, but Christianity is not the project's default frame or destination.

Do not present a later Christian custom as an original pagan practice. When pre-Christian origins are uncertain, say so plainly. Distinguish direct evidence, later folklore, scholarly interpretation, modern reconstruction, and personal meaning.

## Audience and guide
The brand is inclusive of both genders. “Wise Woman” describes Chels’s role as the medicine woman and guide at the head of the project, not the audience’s identity.

## Working product line
Keep the line shallow and distinct from Kitchen & Kettle:

1. **The Old Ways Almanac** — the main seasonal guide, built around seasonal questions, source notes, practical observations, and what is worth carrying forward.
2. **Hearth & Hedgerow Cards** — annotated cards about plants, objects, practices, seasonal signs, words, beliefs, and material culture.
3. **Lore Papers** — short digital investigations built around one focused question.
4. **Field Archive Notebook** — a field journal for plants, weather, animals, household knowledge, sources, and personal observations.
5. **Apothecary and specimen record sheets** — companion downloads for inventories, labels, tags, preparations, and sources.
6. **One combined physical Field Archive / Apothecary Kit** — notebook, labels, herb tags, preparation cards, specimen envelopes, and eventually pressing materials if fulfillment stays manageable.

Fermentation, natural dyes, herbal preparations, and similar subjects may appear inside an Almanac or Lore Paper. They are not separate brand pillars or a generic homesteading product line.

## Launch boundary
Start with the digital products and one physical kit prototype. Do not launch separate kits, individual component listings, or a broad catalog until the first physical product remains manageable after the third unit.
