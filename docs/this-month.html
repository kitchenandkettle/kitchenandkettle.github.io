August

The preserving month

The garden is still generous, but the season has turned. August is for putting away what you will miss in January — without turning every evening into a project.

What's in season now

Tomatoes, peppers, green beans, cucumbers, basil, and the last heavy run of summer berries. Zucchini is still arriving with no regard for how many you already have. The first apples may be showing up, too — tart, crisp, and gone quickly.

What to put by

Tomatoes are the thing to put by now. Freeze them whole if you do not want a canning project, or make one good batch of sauce and stop there. Green beans freeze well after a quick blanch. Herbs can go into pesto, chopped into ice-cube trays with oil, or dried on a rack.

If you have hens, keep preserving the extra eggs. The freezer is useful, and so is a simple plan for using them before the next dozen arrives.

Kitchen rhythm

Cook one thing in quantity, then let it carry a few meals. A pot of beans, a tray of roasted peppers, or a bowl of tomato sauce is more useful than a new dinner plan every night.

If dinner is sliced tomatoes with salt, bread, and whatever cheese is in the fridge, that counts. You are feeding people. It is enough.

Around the house

Make one shelf in the pantry or freezer easier to use before you fill it again. Throw out the mystery container, group the things you actually reach for, and write down what needs using first.

Keep the cleaning routine small. August already has enough work in it.

Guides worth your time
